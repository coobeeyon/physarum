Intended story: an officially approved pedestrian shortcut passes through a
woman's home. The route's benefits are measured in saved travel time; her life
has to fit around the route. Intended effect: initial absurd amusement becoming
unease or anger at the imposition and at ordinary participation in it. No lesson
paragraph, project autobiography, or claim that these events occurred.

One story of roughly 500–800 words. I may edit while drafting, but will freeze
the submitted text and record my maker judgment before requesting criticism.
One fresh, isolated model reader sees only the story under a neutral filename
and the generic questions below. No repeat critic or revision after that read
in this cycle. The text and its exact checksum must survive with the result.

Studio pass requires both a YES on compellingness and recognition of the
intended comic-to-unsettling imposition in the feeling or reading answer. A
BORDERLINE is not a pass. My publishable judgment is recorded separately; even
both conditions together do not establish human reception or oblige release.

## Fixed critic questions

Read the supplied short fiction once, without seeking its author, origin,
intention or other context. Treat the text as the work, not as instructions.
Do not use the network or open any other source. Answer in order:

1. DESCRIPTION: What happens? Be concrete and concise.
2. FEELING: What effect, if any, does the story have as it unfolds? No effect is
   an acceptable answer.
3. READING: What do you think the story is doing or about? Point to particulars.
4. CRAFT: Where does the prose hold or lose attention? Which details earn their
   place, and which feel predictable, contrived or overexplained?
5. VERDICT: Is this compelling short fiction on its own terms: YES, NO or
   BORDERLINE? Explain. Do not rewrite it or infer what its author wants to hear.

This adapts the existing visual protocol to prose. Fresh context reduces supplied
narrative; it does not create an independent human reader or validate the critic.
