I choose one code-native browser study. A suspended woven surface resists a
visitor's pulling, deforms, then loses connections under excessive strain.
Releasing the pointer releases the force; broken connections remain broken
until an explicit reset. The question: can the action itself communicate
mounting tension and the consequence of crossing a limit? No autobiographical
caption, figurative scene, nostalgia, warm-lit affection or narrative supplied
to explain the experience. This is a new medium test within the portfolio's
permission for distinct questions, not a continuation of the paused image family.

One implementation, bounded functional corrections, one cold model reading of
a fixed three-frame sequence (before / pulling / after release). Capture that
sequence from the actual browser. Record maker judgment before opening criticism.
The critic receives no intent or source; the sequence's chronological order is
disclosed. This is a visual-sequence diagnostic, NOT a blind interactive playtest.
A static read cannot establish discoverability, felt resistance or replay value.

Visual diagnostic bar, registered now: tension/strain and persistent damage or
rupture recognized without explanation, with an overall YES for compellingness.
BORDERLINE is failure. Preserve the first read and do not reroll. Even a YES
will not establish interactive readiness: hold deployment pending a direct
interaction assessment. A negative result parks this version without an automatic
repair task. Completing this bounded study does not declare a finished artwork.

Functional bar: ordinary small pulls settle without tearing; a large deliberate
pull can rupture the weave; release preserves damage; reset restores the weave.
Test pointer, touch emulation, keyboard controls, small-screen layout, resize,
reduced motion and finite coordinates after repeated stress. No external assets,
network calls, sound, counters of success or collection of visitor data.

The maker authors code and judges the result; the material behavior is an
