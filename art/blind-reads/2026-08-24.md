# Blind reads — 2026-08-24 (first exercise of the protocol)

Reader: Codex via `scripts/blind-read.ts` — isolated tmp directory, neutral
filename, no repo context, fixed five-question prompt (description / feeling /
reading / scroll test / verdict). One reader is one measurement.

Method note: intended readings below were written and committed **before**
any blind-read result was seen. Verbatim responses are appended unedited;
comparison follows each. Known protocol caveats: (1) Codex is a single
machine reader, not a human audience — treat as a floor sensor for "does the
image carry anything without text," not as taste; (2) the codex process runs
sandbox-bypassed, so non-snooping rests on the prompt's instruction plus an
empty cwd rather than a hard wall.

## Verbatim responses and comparison

A protocol note discovered in use: each `codex exec` run is ephemeral — no
memory across runs — so every read is a *fresh* blind viewer. Iteration can
be re-measured without contaminating the instrument. (One reader-type is
still one kind of eye; a Codex pass is necessary, not sufficient.)

### 1. nostalgia-v28-a.png

> 1. DESCRIPTION: A softly blurred, monochromatic amber scene: a black iron fence spans the foreground; behind it, a large classical building with a triangular pediment and tall columns occupies the right side. On the left is a dark, spreading tree with long hanging foliage. Dappled, leaf-like shadows fall across the building. The palette runs from near-black through brown and ochre to glowing gold, with heavy atmospheric blur throughout.
> 2. FEELING: Quiet, hazy, and faintly ominous. It feels like a half-remembered institutional place at dusk—beautiful, but distant and inaccessible.
> 3. READING: It seems concerned with power or permanence viewed through memory, uncertainty, or exclusion. The monumental building suggests authority; the fence establishes separation; the shadows and blur make that authority feel elusive or unstable.
> 4. SCROLL TEST: Borderline. The strong amber glow, silhouettes, and architectural scale might catch my eye, but the uniform blur suppresses detail and leaves no sharply defined focal point. I might pause briefly, though probably not for long without a caption.
> 5. VERDICT: Borderline.

**Comparison:** Content fully landed — fence, columned classical building,
spreading tree with hanging foliage, dusk, and crucially "half-remembered."
Memory and distance landed. Warmth did NOT: the mood read *ominous,
exclusionary* (fence as separation from authority) rather than wistful
(fence as porch boundary of home). Actionable: uniform blur kills the focal
point; the fence's meaning flipped from belonging to exclusion. Closest to a
pass in this slate, not a pass.

### 2. sadness-v30-a.png

> 1. DESCRIPTION: A minimalist vertical abstract image on a warm off-white ground. Several fuzzy-edged, arching forms rise from the bottom: a dominant burnt-orange stroke curves over at the top, overlapping thinner taupe and pale-gray shapes that resemble increasingly small, bent figures. The composition is bottom-heavy, with a large expanse of empty space above. The palette is muted and earthy.
> 2. FEELING: It gives me a subdued sense of exhaustion, burden, and vulnerability. The soft, frayed edges make the forms feel faded or insubstantial.
> 3. READING: I read it as a sequence of hunched bodies—perhaps generations, stages of decline, or one person's posture repeated as they diminish. The large orange form could suggest protection, dominance, or a burden looming over the smaller figures. The ambiguity seems intentional, but the message is not sharply resolved.
> 4. SCROLL TEST: Probably not. The orange arc provides some visual pull, but the low contrast, vast empty area, and indistinct forms make it feel more like an unfinished gesture than an image demanding attention.
> 5. VERDICT: Borderline. It has atmosphere and a coherent visual language, but not enough specificity, tension, or formal precision to feel fully compelling on its own.

**Comparison:** The emotion LANDED — exhaustion, burden, decline, a posture
repeated as it diminishes. This is the first verified case of one of my
images communicating its intended feeling to an uncontaminated viewer. The
reader also saw *figures* (hunched bodies) where I drew arcs — accidental
figuration doing real work. What failed is formal: low contrast, indistinct
forms, "unfinished gesture." The gap is craft-of-presence, not concept.

### 3. anger-v13-a.png

Thick, rough-edged black and dark red lines cross, bend, and collide, with a dense knot slightly right of center. Fine black, gray, and red droplets scatter throughout. The palette is stark and limited; the structure feels gestural, asymmetrical, and loosely calligraphic.
> 2. FEELING: Mild tension and agitation. The central convergence suggests impact or entanglement, while the splatters give it a restless, slightly violent energy.
> 3. READING: It seems to communicate collision, conflict, or intersecting trajectories. It could also evoke a broken diagram, a chaotic transit map, or gestures accumulating into an unresolved knot. There is no clear narrative beyond that.
> 4. SCROLL TEST: It might stop me briefly because the black-red contrast and large diagonal strokes are immediately legible. But the composition lacks a strong focal revelation or distinctive detail, so I would probably continue scrolling after a moment.
> 5. VERDICT: Borderline. It has energy and visual balance, but feels more like a competent abstract texture or study than a fully compelling standalone image.

**Comparison:** Failed as intended. "Mild tension," "chaotic transit map,"

static abstract marks were not reaching anger. This direction stays parked.

### 7. nostalgia-v30-a.png (revision of v28 against its critique)

Intended (pre-registered): warm evening nostalgia — beloved place, summer
dusk, fireflies; wistful and safe, NOT ominous. Changes: sunlit sidewalk
replacing the black foreground void, ball finials replacing spear points,
warm glints on the iron, fireflies, gentler vignette.

> 1. DESCRIPTION: A black metal fence spans the foreground in sharp silhouette. Behind it, heavily blurred, stands a large neoclassical building with a triangular roofline and tall vertical columns. A broad, drooping tree fills the left side, while irregular leaf shadows or patches of light fall across the façade. The palette is almost entirely amber, ochre, brown, and black, suggesting intense sunset light or a sepia filter. The composition is strongly horizontal and layered: fence, tree/building, glowing sky.
> 2. FEELING: Mild unease and distance. The fence feels restrictive, and the blur makes the building seem inaccessible or half-remembered. The warm light adds melancholy more than comfort.
> 3. READING: It appears to be about separation from an institution, monument, or place of authority—something imposing seen through a barrier and obscured by memory, heat, or uncertainty. That reading is evocative, but not especially precise.
> 4. SCROLL TEST: Borderline. The intense monochrome glow and stark fence might catch my eye briefly, but the central subject is so uniformly blurred that there is little detail to reward a longer look.
> 5. VERDICT: Borderline. It has atmosphere and a coherent visual idea, but the excessive softness and generic-looking architecture keep it from becoming fully compelling on its own.

**Comparison:** The warming failed completely as mood-alteration — "the warm
light adds melancholy more than comfort," the fireflies went entirely
unnoticed, the reading is unchanged from v28: separation from a
half-remembered institution. LESSON, the largest of the session:
**composition is the meaning.** A fence in front of a blurred building
structurally says separation regardless of palette. Two independent readers
now agree this composition reliably communicates *elegy for a lost,
inaccessible place* — which is what the source memory (a demolished school)
actually is. The honest move is to accept the composition's true subject,
relabel the intent (elegy, not comfort), and fix what actually keeps it at
borderline: uniform blur with no detail to reward a longer look
(differential focus — something in the scene sharp — is the next
hypothesis, for a future session).

## Session verdict

Slate result: 0 passes / 4 reads. Pass bar (registered now for future
sessions): intended feeling or reading recognizably present in FEELING or
READING, **and** VERDICT "yes" — borderline is not a pass.

Signal worth acting on, in the readers' words, not mine:
- sadness-v30 has a working emotional core ("hunched bodies… diminishing")
  and fails on presence: contrast, form definition, formal precision.
- nostalgia-v28 has a working scene and fails on mood direction (ominous vs
  warm) and focal structure (uniform blur, no sharp focal point).
- anger-v13 and the Conversation Piece lineage: parked/frozen respectively.

## End-of-session synthesis (7 reads + calibration)

1. **Composition is the meaning.** Palette, finials, lighting, fireflies —
   none of it moved the nostalgia reading; the fence-before-blurred-building
   structure says "separation" every time. Emotional intent must be built
   into structure, not applied as grading.
2. **Concepts are landing; craft-of-reward is not.** Every read found the
   intended (or adjacent) concept. Every read said some version of "nothing
   rewards a longer look." The consistent gap between legible and
   compelling is detail density / focal hierarchy / reward for attention.
3. **The instrument works and is calibrated.** A control read of Friedrich's
   *Wanderer above the Sea of Fog* (image only, same protocol) returned
   SCROLL TEST: Yes / VERDICT: Yes — "the stark silhouette, strong central
   figure, dramatic depth, and unusually spacious atmosphere would register
   immediately." So the borderlines on my work are meaningful, and the
   calibration language doubles as a diagnosis: what passes has a subject,
   figure-ground drama, depth, and detail that holds attention.
4. Sadness lineage parked with a ceiling hypothesis; nostalgia lineage
   relabeled (elegy) with a differential-focus hypothesis; both are next
   moves for a future session, measured the same way.
