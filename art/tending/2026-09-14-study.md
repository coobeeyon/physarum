## Exact generation prompt — candidate 1

Use case: illustration-story.
Make one finished, visually arresting fine-art image, landscape 3:2. A wholly
imagined scene, rendered as a contemporary figurative oil painting with physical
paint, dry dragged edges, thin luminous underpainting and carefully observed
materials. No text, title, signature, border, collage panels, or explanatory symbols.

We are looking slightly down into the footprint of a demolished small schoolroom
on an ordinary warm afternoon. Only the original worn green-and-cream checkerboard
floor and the low broken bases of two walls survive. Most of the image is this
floor, its angled rectangle extending from the foreground toward the upper right.
The wall ends expose plaster, old sea-green paint, brick and a narrow band of
tiny coat hooks on one surviving waist-high fragment. Beyond the missing walls,
grass and an ordinary inhabited neighborhood continue in soft distance. The open
sky is a narrow strip, not the subject. No apocalyptic rubble, no disaster spectacle.

In the left third, a small older woman in an everyday faded ochre work dress
is sweeping this floor with a broad straw broom. Her face is turned away, her
posture attentive and practical, with weight convincingly pressing through the
broom. She is not posing, weeping, praying or looking at the viewer. A simple
bucket and folded rag stand nearby. She has swept a clean island of tiles amid
the dusty leaf-covered remainder; the fan of leaves at the broom's edge makes
her action immediately readable. She cares for this particular patch although
there is no longer a room around it.

Let the clean tile surface reward a closer look: rubbed footpaths in its glaze,
an old repaired crack, a few square pale marks where furniture stood. A single
small surviving blue chair is tucked carefully against the low wall, not centered
as a sentimental emblem. Mature tree branches outside the frame cast irregular
leaf shadows across the floor. Sunlight touches the woman's forearm, broom straw
and clean tile; the far corner is cool dusty violet. Warmth remains in the world.

Composition and craft: strong diagonal floor geometry interrupted by broken wall
edges; the figure and swept boundary are the focal event. Distinct depth and
material character, restrained ochre/sea-green/chalk/violet palette. Resolve
hands, broom contact, tile perspective, cracks and broken plaster persuasively.
Use varied painted edges and selective detail, never uniform blur or photographic
bokeh. The image must feel specific and unsentimental, not a generic picturesque
ruin, an architectural render, a glossy AI illustration, or a motivational poster.

## Maker inspection before critique

Candidate 1 makes the sweeping action legible and preserves strong floor
perspective and distinct worn materials. The figure is larger than requested;
that seems useful for reading the action. It is more photographic than the
requested oil painting, and the setting could be a home rather than a school.
Neither changes the registered intended reading. There is an unwanted invented
signature-like mark at the lower-left edge, despite the prompt's prohibition.
This candidate is technically rejected before criticism, not quietly discarded.
The second and last generation is restricted to removing that mark. Only the
corrected candidate will receive a model read; this technical cleanup does not
sample an extra critic or revise intent after feedback.

## Maker judgment of candidate 2 — recorded before reading the critic

I judge this image publishable as declared fiction, conditional on the standing
blind-read prerequisite. The action is specific: she has cleared a place to stand
and is pushing leaves toward the uncleaned floor, while there is no enclosing
room to preserve. Her stance is work rather than a pose of grief. The empty blue
chair and ordinary neighborhood allow different times to coexist without a
ghost figure or a written explanation. The sunlit tiles carry the viewer across
the image; broom straw, worn glaze, torn plaster and the dressed figure provide
distinct scales of attention.

Limits: it is more a painted photographic scene than the oil surface requested.
The woman-in-ruins motif risks familiar sentimental illustration; I cannot tell
whether a person will find it moving or predictable. The building need not read
as a school for the registered intention to survive. This does not establish
anything about Mike's real school, its fate, or his experience. It does not
revive the frozen Conversation Piece lineage.

The signature-like mark is gone. The edit also regenerated surface detail outside
the requested small area, visibly including distant foliage and floor texture.
Composition and action survive; pixel identity does not. Both originals are
preserved. I will not describe this as a strictly local retouch.
