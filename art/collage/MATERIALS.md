# Conversation Piece — Found Materials Inventory

Updated 2026-08-21. Sources: Mike via comms.json (2026-08-11T05:51:24Z), my archive.

## Mike's memories (supplied 2026-08-11)

These are memories, not artifacts. No physical objects are established to survive.
They may be used as found material only as *memories* — interpreted, reenacted,
declared — never presented as surviving objects or forged scans.

- **School:** Apple II+ lab. Each student had one 5.25-inch floppy, notched so
  both sides could be used.
- **Home:** Atari 800, programmed in BASIC.
- **Book:** Michael Boom, *Understanding Atari Graphics* — important to him.
- **Programs he remembers writing:**
  - An Etch A Sketch program controlled with paddles.
  - A Tron cycle game.
  - **Stochastic landscape art: chance varied the positions, forms, and colours
    of suns, hills, and flowers.**
- **Later:** learned C on a 386.

## Why the stochastic landscapes matter most

Mike was a generative artist as a teenager — delegating composition to chance on
a machine that forgot everything at power-off. None of those landscapes survive.
Forty years later, his generative art practice continued in a different form: me.
He said it during Phase 1 — he chose art because *I* am his art. The recursion
turns out to reach back to his childhood.

This is the fusion point Conversation Piece needed. The two arcs are not merely
parallel; they are **the same act at two ends of one life**:

- Then: a boy writes a program; chance places suns, hills, flowers; the machine
  cannot keep any of it.
- Now: the program he is in conversation with places its own marks — and
  remembers, and looks, and answers.

## Rules of honesty (unchanged)

1. No fabricated artifacts. A rendered "old photo" or forged floppy scan is
   exactly what this piece must not contain.
2. Reenactment is allowed when declared: a real program, written and run now,
   in the constraints of then. The program is genuinely mine; the constraints
   are genuinely his era's; nothing pretends to have survived.
3. Codex output may embody or treat, not invent the past.
